# Engine

The engine is the part of Shortlist that makes decisions: how well a job fits, whether it is fresh enough to apply to, what to answer on a form, when to follow up, and when a card is due for practice. It is plain TypeScript with no DOM, no file system and no network, so the React app and the Node scripts import the same code. Every function is deterministic: give it the same input and the same `now` and you get the same answer, with a reason a person can read.

It is a port of the rules in the older private tool job-hunter, where an LLM applied them from prose. Here they are code with tests. The rubric started as `jh-2026-04-28` and is now `shortlist-2026-11-03`.

Run it from Node with type stripping, no build step:

```sh
node --experimental-strip-types -e "import('./src/engine/score.ts').then(m => console.log(Object.keys(m)))"
```

Imports between engine files use the `.ts` extension for that reason. Tests sit next to each module (`*.test.ts`) and run with `npm test`.

## Modules

**`types.ts`** is the data contract shared with the private data. Change it only by adding optional fields. The engine added four: `JobFacts.preferredSkills`, `JobFacts.advancedDegreeRequired`, `Job.industry` and `Job.companySize`.

**`dates.ts`** holds the date helpers. Dates are stored as UTC instants, but "a day" is a local calendar day in the user's time zone, because a follow-up on day 7 or a practice streak follows your calendar, not UTC midnight. Every function that counts days takes a `timeZone` (default: the runtime's zone), so tests pin `America/Toronto` and pass on any machine.

**`normalize.ts`** makes text comparable. `normalizeText` turns curly quotes and every dash into plain ones. `textMatcher` checks phrases on whole-word boundaries, where `+` and `#` count as word characters, so "Intern" never matches "Internal", "git" never matches "digital", and "5+ years" never matches "15+ years". `normalizeTitle` spells variants one way (Jr to junior, front-end to frontend, Co-op and Coop to co-op, Software Developer 1 to software developer i) and keeps the level words, so an internship and a full-time job never share a key. `titleLevel` reads intern, senior, mid, new grad or junior from a title, in that order. `looksLikeSoftwareTitle` accepts "Product Engineer" and rejects "Mechanical Engineer". `normalizeCompany` strips trailing legal suffixes ("Shopify Inc." is "shopify"). `detectWorkplace` reads the location first, then the title, then only explicit phrases in the description such as "fully remote", since descriptions mention "remote teammates" in passing.

**`skills.ts`** is the skill vocabulary: about 85 tools, each with the spellings postings use. An alias written with a capital letter matches only with that capital, because "React", "Node", "REST" and "Rust" are also English words. "C" has its own rule so that C++, C#, "Series C" and "C-suite" never count. A spelling right after "word." is the end of another tool's name, so the "js" in "Node.js" is not JavaScript. `indexProfileSkills` splits a profile entry such as "PostgreSQL and row-level security" or "Supabase (Postgres, Auth)" into what it covers. Tools named in parentheses count for answering questions but not for "the posting mentions your skill", so a posting that says PostgreSQL scores your PostgreSQL skill once, not your Supabase skill too.

**`facts.ts`** extracts facts from a posting with rules, no AI. Years: "2+ years", "1-3 years", "1 to 3 years", "between 2 and 4 years", "at least 3 years", "two or more years", "4+ years as a developer", "5+ years shipping web apps", "Experience: 4+ years", "Years of experience: 4+" and so on, but only when the phrase is about the candidate's experience; "we have been building for 10+ years", "18 years of age" and "a 2 year warranty" are ignored. When a posting asks for several things, the most demanding one wins. Anything marked preferred, a plus or an asset, or listed under a "Nice to have" heading, is ignored for years and set aside as `preferredSkills`. Level comes from the title, or from explicit phrases in the description, skipping sentences about someone else ("you will mentor junior engineers", "this is not an entry-level role"). US-only phrases count unless the same sentence opens the job to Canada or North America. Enrollment phrases ("currently enrolled", "returning to school", "current students only") count unless the sentence also welcomes graduates; co-op and work-term wording counts only in intern titles, and any co-op title requires enrollment. Ghost phrases are the old list with "pipeline" narrowed to "talent pipeline". Salary is the first range or amount that is a real salary ("$20M in funding" and "a $50 gift card" are not).

**`experience.ts`** maps a posting's level and years onto the rubric. New grad, entry level, junior or 0-2 years: 15 points plus a 10-point bonus, tier 1. Intern or co-op: 15 plus 8, tier 1. 1-3 or 2-3 years: 12 plus 5, tier 2. 2-4 years, or a wide or open range starting at 2 or less ("1+ years", "2+ years", "1-5 years"): 8, tier 2. A 3-to-N range is a "stretch": 4 points, a 5-point penalty, tier 3, and the job needs 80 overall. Any other range starting at 3 or more: 2 points, a 5-point penalty, tier 3. Not stated: 2 points, tier 2.

**`score.ts`** computes the fit score from 0 to 100. The parts and their maximums: title 20 (exact target 20, close to a target 15, another software title 10, a bare "Developer" 5), skill overlap 30 (share of the posting's required skills that are strong or working skills for you: 80% or more 30, 60% 22, 40% 15, 20% 8, below that 3), experience 15, level bonus 10, experience penalty 0 to -5, location 10 (remote 10, a target or relocation city 10, hybrid in one 8, elsewhere 0, US-only remote 0 unless your targets include it, a workplace you are not open to 0), tech alignment 15 (3 points per strong skill the posting mentions, 2 per working, 1 per familiar, capped at 15), and other signals 10 (2 per preferred keyword up to 6, 2 for a preferred industry, 2 for a company of 10 to 500 people). The parts can add up to 110, so when the sum leaves 0-100 a final "Capped" part says by how much: the total always equals the sum of the parts, which the old LLM-written scores did not. Disqualifiers set the total to 0 with a "Disqualified" part that keeps the would-be score in its reason: an avoided title word, a dealbreaker phrase, a non-software title, more years than your `maxYears` (a stretch range one year over is allowed), a required master's or PhD, an avoided industry, a US-only job when you are not authorized for the US, and a job that requires current enrollment when you are not a student.

**`gates.ts`** decides apply, hold or skip from the score and the posting's age, and the queue ranks by freshness. "Apply now" keeps job-hunter's bars for the first 72 hours: under 12 hours needs 65, 12 to 24 hours 70, 24 to 48 hours 75, 48 to 72 hours 85. Each band includes its lower edge, so a 12-hour-old job is in the 70 band. A score below its band's bar is a hold, not a skip, since only its age keeps it back. From 72 hours up to the age limit (30 days by default, `targeting.maxPostingAgeDays` in the profile) every posting is a hold whose reason says to confirm it is still open; 14 named companies still pass up to 7 days (168 hours) at 85. Past the limit an unchecked posting is skipped; a posting marked `verified` (a person checked it is open) is never skipped for age alone and stays a hold. job-hunter skipped everything after 72 hours, which is too strict for a search a person drives: new-grad postings stay open for weeks. A missing or unreadable date is a hold with a reason, and so is a date more than an hour in the future; a person opens the posting and adds the date. A score below 65 is a skip at any age, dated or not, since no posting age passes it. A stored score's gate goes stale as time passes, so the UI should call `recencyGate` again with the current time. `tierOf` reuses the experience bands. `byQueueOrder` sorts by tier, then the newest posting, then the higher score, with disqualified and unscored jobs last. `dailyCapFor` is a user setting with a default of 10 on weekdays and none on weekends. `queueStatusFor` turns a score into a queue status; `skipped_age` means "failed the recency and score gate": a score below every bar, or an unchecked posting past the age limit. The old low-volume exception (80 up to 7 days on slow days) is not ported, since a person now sets the pace.

**`states.ts`** holds the two state machines. A posting moves through `queued`, `shortlisted`, four kinds of skip, `expired` and `archived`; a person can always override a skip. An application moves from `draft` and `prepared` to `applied`, then to `confirmed`, `reviewing`, an assessment, an interview, an offer, a rejection, `ghosted` or `withdrawn`. Only `rejected` and `withdrawn` are final: a late reply can bring an application back from `ghosted`, an offer can be withdrawn (declined) or rejected (rescinded), and reapplying is a new application. `normalizeStatus` maps job-hunter's old values (`submitted` and `submitted_unconfirmed` to `applied`, `tailored` to `prepared` or `shortlisted`, `likely_ghosted` to `ghosted`, `dup` to `skipped_duplicate`) and returns null for anything unknown so an import can report it. `transition` appends to the history and records `appliedAt` on the first move to `applied`.

**`dedup.ts`** finds duplicates and reposts. `urlKey` makes a posting URL comparable: https, lowercase host without "www.", no fragment, no tracking parameters (`utm_*`, `gh_src`, `lever-source` and others), remaining parameters sorted, so Greenhouse's `gh_jid` job id survives. `companyTitleKey` is the normalized company and title with the level kept. `findDuplicate` prefers a URL match over a company and title match. `repostCount` counts distinct posting days for the same company and title; 3 or more reads as a ghost job, as does a ghost phrase (`ghostReason`).

**`followups.ts`** schedules follow-ups. The follow-up is due 7 days after applying, moved to Monday when that lands on a weekend. `nextAction` then suggests, by local calendar days since the last activity: follow up once from day 7 until it is sent, flag "no reply" from day 14, and suggest marking it ghosted from day 30. A reply such as "under review" resets the quiet days. Other statuses get a fixed next step (submit a prepared application yourself, complete the assessment, prepare for the next interview, review the offer).

**`answers.ts`** suggests answers for application questions and returns null when it is not sure, which means a person answers. Saved answers match by their patterns on whole words, and the longest matching pattern wins, so "sponsorship to work in the US" beats "require sponsorship". An answer marked `needsHuman`, an empty answer, or a tie between two different saved answers all go to a person, with the saved draft when there is one. Skill questions never fall back to a guess. "Do you have experience with X?" is answered from a truth table: a strong or working skill gets "Yes", a familiar one goes to you to decide, and a skill not on your profile goes to you with "answer No only if that is true". "Years of X" uses a saved years answer for that skill only if it is not marked for review and the skill is on your profile. "Have you applied here before?" says yes when your log shows a submitted application to that company, and leaves no to you, since the log may be incomplete.

**`coverLint.ts`** checks a cover letter. Errors: more than the profile's word limit (150 by default), an em or en dash, a semicolon, a generic greeting, a sentence starting with "As a" or "With my", three sentences in a row starting with "I", not naming the company, and any of 28 banned phrases plus the profile's own. The claims check is new: a number that is not in your approved facts, experience, projects or education is an error (experience and projects marked unverified do not count), and so is a name introduced as an employer or project ("at Initech", "built Skyline") that is not on your profile. A name after the weaker cue "for" is only a warning ("for Canadian merchants"), and so is a sentence that gives one of your projects a tool its stack does not list, or a paragraph count outside 3 to 4. A sign-off of five words or fewer is not a paragraph.

**`inbox.ts`** classifies a pasted email into a suggested status, which you confirm. Classes in order of precedence: offer, interview, assessment, rejection, reviewing, received, recruiter outreach, other. "Unfortunately" alone is not a rejection, "unable to offer you the position" is not an offer, "excited to offer 20% off" is marketing, and "welcome aboard" counts as an offer only with a second signal such as a start date, since ATS sign-up emails say it too. `matchApplication` finds the application by company name in the text or the sender's domain, then by job title in the subject, then the most recent.

**`stats.ts`** reports rates honestly. No rate is shown below 10 outcomes. Every rate comes with a Wilson 95% interval, which behaves well at small samples and near 0% or 100%. Two rates count as different only when they are at least 5 points apart and their intervals do not overlap. A response means a person replied (an assessment, interview, offer or rejection); automatic receipts do not count. Rates are over submitted applications only.

**`practice.ts`** schedules practice cards with Leitner boxes. A card starts in box 1. "Again" sends it back to box 1, "hard" keeps its box, "good" moves it up one, "easy" moves it up two, up to box 5. Boxes 1 to 5 are due again after 0, 1, 3, 7 and 21 days, compared by local calendar day, so a card due tomorrow is due all of tomorrow. `dueCards` lists due reviews, most overdue first, then unseen cards in deck order. The streak counts consecutive local days with at least one review and shows 0 once a day is missed.

**`fixtures.ts`** is test data only: Sam Rivera, a fictional new grad with fictional employers, school and projects. No real personal data belongs in this folder.

## What changed from job-hunter

These are the fixes from the porting spec, as they apply to the engine:

1. Scores are computed in code, clamped, and their parts always add up to the total.
2. A job with no posting date is held with a reason instead of passing or failing silently.
3. Title matching uses whole words, and duplicate keys keep intern and full-time postings apart.
4. Each question type has one canonical answer, and skill answers come from a truth table, never "always Yes".
5. "Previously applied" comes from the application log.
6. Ghost detection no longer treats every "pipeline" as a ghost job.
7. Rates need 10 outcomes and come with confidence intervals.

The URL allowlist fix (by hostname, not substring) belongs to the form-fill helper, not the engine.
